Overview

This page describes the beta-binomial extension, which models the extra cell-line-to-cell-line variance directly rather than assuming pure binomial sampling noise.

Sites tested: the same 5-fold union (≥ 1 fold) set, 12,416 sites. Samples: the same 50 genotyped cell lines. Covariates: the same M-value PCs (below).

The model

For a given pU site, let individual \(i\) have \(y_i\) converted reads out of \(n_i\) total reads. The modification rate is itself a random quantity \(p_i\), drawn from a Beta prior whose mean is modelled and whose dispersion is shared across cell lines:

\[ y_i \mid p_i \sim \text{Binomial}(n_i,\, p_i), \qquad p_i \sim \text{Beta}(\mu_i,\, \phi) \] \[ \text{logit}(\mu_i) = \beta_0 + \beta_g G_i + z_i^\top\gamma \]

Here \(\text{Beta}(\mu, \phi)\) is the Beta distribution in mean–dispersion form: \(\mu_i\) is the expected modification rate for cell line \(i\), and \(\phi\) is the over-dispersion. The conventional shape parameters are the derived quantities,

\[ \alpha_i = \mu_i\frac{1-\phi}{\phi}, \qquad \beta_i = (1-\mu_i)\frac{1-\phi}{\phi}, \qquad \mu_i = \frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i+\beta_i}, \qquad \phi = \frac{1}{\alpha_i+\beta_i+1} \]

Marginalizing over \(p_i\) gives the beta-binomial distribution for \(y_i\), with the same mean model as the binomial but a different variance:

\[ \text{Var}(y_i) = n_i\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\big[\,1+(n_i-1)\phi\,\big] \]

  • \(\phi \in [0,1)\) — the overdispersion / intra-class-correlation parameter: how much the \(n_i\) reads at one cell line behave like correlated (not independent) trials. \(\phi=0\) recovers the binomial exactly.
  • The bracketed term \(1+(n_i-1)\phi\) is a variance inflation factor — the same form as the design effect in clustered/survey data. It grows with depth \(n_i\), so high-depth cell lines are not automatically down-weighted as much as the binomial would assume, once \(\phi>0\).
  • One \(\phi\) per site (not per cell line, not per SNP) — a single dispersion shared across the site’s 50 observations.

The latent rate \(p_i\)

\(p_i\) is the quantity the model is really about: cell line \(i\)’s true modification rate, as distinct from the observed fraction \(y_i/n_i\). It is a latent variable, never estimated directly — the likelihood above is obtained by integrating it out — but the fitted model states its full distribution, and the Beta–Binomial conjugacy makes both the prior and the posterior closed form:

\[ \text{prior:}\quad p_i \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha_i,\, \beta_i), \qquad \text{posterior:}\quad p_i \mid y_i \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha_i + y_i,\; \beta_i + n_i - y_i) \]

\[ E[p_i \mid y_i] = \frac{\alpha_i + y_i}{\alpha_i + \beta_i + n_i} = w_i\cdot\frac{y_i}{n_i} + (1-w_i)\cdot\mu_i, \qquad w_i = \frac{n_i}{n_i + \frac{1-\phi}{\phi}} \]

so the posterior mean is a shrinkage estimator interpolating between the cell line’s observed fraction and the fitted mean, with the weight set by depth relative to \(\alpha_i+\beta_i\).

Two structural points follow from the parameterization:

  • \(\alpha_i + \beta_i\) does not depend on \(i\). It equals \((1-\phi)/\phi\), one scalar per site. So \(\alpha_i\) and \(\beta_i\) individually vary with \(\mu_i\), but their sum is fixed — which is what makes a single \(\phi\) well defined, and is the assumption that all cell lines at a site share one dispersion.
  • Both ends of \(\phi\)’s range are degenerate. \(\phi \to 0\) sends \(\alpha_i+\beta_i \to \infty\), collapsing the prior onto \(\mu_i\) and reducing the model to a plain binomial. \(\phi \to 1\) sends \(\alpha_i+\beta_i \to 0\), collapsing the prior onto point masses at 0 and 1 — the model asserting each cell line is either fully modified or not at all. Neither is a meaningful fitted dispersion.

QTL mapping: the beta-binomial LRT

Per (site, cis-SNP) pair, \(\beta_g\) is tested with a likelihood-ratio test: fit the full model (with the cis-SNP) and the covariates-only null model by maximum likelihood (\(\beta_0,\gamma,\phi\) jointly for each), compare log-likelihoods:

\[ \Lambda = 2\big(\log\text{Lik}_{\text{full}} - \log\text{Lik}_{\text{null}}\big) \;\overset{H_0}{\sim}\; \chi^2_1, \qquad H_0:\ \beta_g = 0 \]

nf_null <- fit_bb_mle(y, n, X)         # joint MLE: beta0, gamma, phi
nf_full <- fit_bb_mle(y, n, cbind(X, g))
stat <- max(2 * (nf_full$loglik - nf_null$loglik), 0)
p_snp <- pchisq(stat, df = 1, lower.tail = FALSE)

Why the LRT, matching the binomial model’s decision. A faster Rao score test (fit the null once per site, test every cis-SNP via a vectorized computation, using the exact digamma/trigamma score and information of the beta-binomial log-likelihood — closed form via \(k=(1-\phi)/\phi\), \(a_i=\mu_ik\), \(b_i=(1-\mu_i)k\)) was built and validated first, since a full-model refit per cis-SNP is far more expensive than one null fit per site. It performs better here than the binomial’s score test did — validated against a numerical gradient/Hessian (exact match) and against the real LRT (rank correlation 0.955 on real sites, essentially unbiased) — but the underlying concern is the same structural one that motivated the binomial’s switch: the score test’s variance is a subtraction of two similarly-sized quantities (\(V = G^\top\text{diag}(w)G - (\cdots)\)), which is exposed to cancellation and near-singularity failure modes that a directly-computed log-likelihood comparison isn’t. 0.955 is good, not perfect, and for consistency with the binomial’s LRT-based final results the same test is used here.

Making the LRT affordable. A full-model refit per cis-SNP per permutation was originally estimated at ~200,000+ CPU-hours for a full 24-config scan — infeasible. Two optimizations to fit_bb_mle() (2026-07-27) brought this down substantially: (1) dropping the constant lchoose(n,y) term from the per-iteration objective (it cancels exactly in the LRT statistic anyway) and (2) supplying the optimizer an analytic gradient (reusing the same digamma-based score derivation as the exact score test above) instead of finite-difference — together roughly a 2.5-2.9x speedup, validated against the original implementation’s fitted values on real sites (matching to 1e-4) before being adopted. Combined with running a preliminary pass at 100 permutations (not the full 1000) to rank PC/window configs and check calibration — verified via Monte Carlo to stay unbiased with about twice the noise of 500 permutations, adequate for this purpose — the full 24-config grid now costs ~3,000 CPU-hours total. Permutations will be increased once the best config is chosen.

Note the LRT’s permutation loop no longer needs the fixed-\(\phi\) mean-only-refit shortcut described in the score-test era below — with the optimizer this fast, every permutation just refits the full joint MLE directly.

Significance

Same three-step procedure as the binomial model.

1. Permutation as a calibration check

Same audit as the binomial: permute the phenotype and covariates together across the 50 cell lines (5 seeds; genotype stays fixed), refit fresh (phi re-estimated under each permutation), collect nominal LRT p-values, check \(\lambda\approx1\).

2. Within-site correction (permutation beta-approximation)

Same procedure as the binomial: minimum nominal p across a site’s cis-SNPs, permutation null of that minimum, Beta(a,b) fit, adjusted per-site p from the Beta CDF.

3. Across-site correction

BH-FDR (and q-values) over the per-site adjusted p, same as the binomial.

Model fit across PC counts

The joint MLE (\(k\) PCs + intercept + \(\phi\)) is reliably fittable across the full swept range, \(k\in\{3,5,7,10,15,20\}\): ~7,600 of 12,416 sites fit at k=20 (vs. the binomial’s ~7,900 at the same k). See the results page for the sites-fit table.

Data

  • Sites: 5-fold union, ≥ 1 fold — 12,416 pU sites.
  • Phenotype: per site, per cell line — converted reads \(C\) (\(=y\)) out of total depth \(N\) (\(=C+T\)), identical to the binomial model (no transformation).
  • Genotypes: cis SNPs within a window of each site (±5/20/50/100kb), common variants (MAF≥0.05) from the 50 cell lines’ genotypes.
  • Covariates \(z\): M-value phenotype PCs — identical construction to the binomial model (site-specific \((\pi_s, 1-\pi_s)\) pseudocount), swept over \(k \in \{3,5,7,10,15,20\}\) (reliably fittable across the full range).
