}

Summary

The beta-binomial cis scan produced top hits that a phenotype permutation did not displace — 19 of the 20 strongest sites under permutation were the same sites as the real top 20, none sharing a lead SNP. Permutation is supposed to destroy all genotype–phenotype signal, so those sites were being selected for some reason other than genotype.

26 sites are responsible. This page describes what they are and what removing them does to calibration.

  • 14 of the 26 carry a U↔︎C sequence variant on the pU base itself, which makes the measured “%pU” partly a read-out of genotype instead of modification (§2.2).
  • 4 more show the identical pattern with no variant called at the base — consistent with the same cause, but not confirmed.
  • 8 are a different problem: sites with almost no modification where a single cell line supplies nearly all converted reads (§2.3).
  • Removing the 26 collapses the QQ tail from 289 to 40; removing every U/C site instead is what fixes λ (§3).

1. How the 26 sites were found

The cis scan was rerun six times — once on the real data, once under each of five phenotype permutations (set.seed(1000 + k), phenotype and covariates reindexed together, genotypes untouched) — this time keeping the site identity attached to every p-value, which the original calibration script discarded.

Under permutation no genotype–phenotype relationship exists, so any site still returning an extreme p-value there is broken on its own terms. Two criteria:

2.2 Mechanism A: a U↔︎C variant on the pU base

What the problem is

Pseudouridine is a modified uridine — chemically still a U, and the assay detects it by reading the base at that position and scoring conversions. The readout is therefore a statement about what base is there, and it cannot distinguish two different reasons for seeing something other than a plain U:

  1. the U was modified to pseudouridine — the biology we want to measure; or
  2. the cell line inherited a C at that position instead of a U.

When a common sequence variant changes that exact base from U to C, the two are indistinguishable. A cell line homozygous for the C allele reads as ~100% “modified” at every read; a heterozygote reads ~50%, because half its transcripts carry the C; a homozygous-reference cell line reads whatever the true modification rate is. The phenotype becomes a genotype assay.

That gives three consequences, all visible below:

  • %pU clusters into three discrete levels near 0%, 50% and 100% rather than varying continuously;
  • those levels track the variant almost perfectly, so any SNP in linkage with it predicts the phenotype — a real association, but of the wrong thing; and
  • the effect does not weaken with depth, because it is not a sampling artefact.

Written against the genome this appears as C/T on the + strand and A/G on the − strand — the same U→C change on the transcript, seen from whichever strand the transcript came from. Genome-wide, 1,118 sites carry such a variant.

2.3 Mechanism B: one cell line supplies nearly all the converted reads

These sites have essentially no modification — pooled %pU between 1.2% and 8.8%. Most cell lines report zero converted reads. Then one cell line reports a large %pU, and it alone accounts for up to 98% of every converted read at the site.

Removing the 26 fixes the tail. The largest -log10 p falls from 289.1 to 40.2 in the real data and from 285 to 12.4 in the permutations — so essentially all of the manufactured signal lived in those 26 sites. λ barely moves (2.473 → 2.43), because λ is computed from the median statistic and 26 sites out of ~11,000 cannot shift a median.

Removing all U/C sites fixes λ. The opposite pattern: λ drops to 1.402, a much larger change, while the tail stays at 179.8 — because the four sites with no variant call (§2.2) are not in the U/C list and are among the worst offenders.

Neither exclusion alone does both. Worth noting for interpretation: the ~1,100 U/C sites that are not among the 26 are not a calibration failure at all — permutation removes their signal normally. They are a problem because the phenotype is a genotype read-out, so a statistically sound association is being reported as a modification QTL.

Caveats

  • The four sites with no variant call are assigned to mechanism A on the strength of their %pU shape. Confirming that needs base-level sequence at those positions, not more statistics.
  • A large-effect QTL and aliasing look alike. If a variant largely abolished modification, non-carriers would sit high, homozygous carriers near zero and heterozygotes between — the same three bands. So banding is a reason to check the base, not a verdict on its own. This matters for the ordinary hits in §2.4 as well; the companion page quantifies how many are affected.
  • The exclusion has not been re-run. These figures filter the existing results. Rebuilding the phenotype matrix without the excluded sites would also change the multiple-testing correction.
  • Assay direction unconfirmed. The U/C reading assumes pseudouridine is detected at the same base the variant changes; worth confirming with whoever ran the protocol.
